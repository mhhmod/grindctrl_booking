02, the landing page

The landing page is a paced product story followed by an ordinary scrolling section. The story has five full screen scenes that slide over each other like sheets: Try it on, Live store, AI operations, The product and Results. Each scene has a few beats. One scroll gesture, one arrow key press or one swipe moves exactly one beat, so a visitor can barely read and still understand what GrindCTRL does, because the screen shows it. After the last beat the page scrolls normally through the stack map, How it works, the closing band and the footer. The screenshots in `design/screens/landing` show every beat in order in all four layouts. The runtime reference is `design/prototype/logic/director11.js` together with the logic class inside `design/prototype/boards/Scroll.dc.html`. Port that behaviour, don't reinvent it.

The page as a whole. `app/page.tsx` keeps its server work: it resolves the locale and wraps everything in `LandingLocaleProvider` with the golden root class. Inside, `components/landing/story/landing-story.tsx` is a client component that renders the fixed header, the five scene sections, the rest of the page and the footer. It replaces the section list in `components/landing/site-landing.tsx`. Components that nothing uses any more can then be deleted in the same pull request. The five scenes have stable ids used for deep links: `try`, `store`, `ops`, `product` and `results`. The rest has `stack`, `journey` and, on phones, `chat`. Opening `/#store` from anywhere lands on the first beat of that scene, and `/#try` lands on the first try-on beat after the hero, just like the Try it on nav item. Read the hash once the stop positions have been measured, and again on every `hashchange`, which also covers client-side navigation from another page to `/#store`. Map the old hashes `#how`, `#demo` and `#benefits` to `#journey`, `#try` and `#product`.

How the story is laid out. One thing must come first. The guarded root class includes `overflow-x-hidden`, which turns the landing root into a scroll container, and `position: sticky` then sticks to that container instead of the viewport, so nothing pins. Keep the class string exactly as the guard test wants it, and add an unlayered rule `.gc-landing-root { overflow-x: clip; }` to `app/globals.css`. Unlayered rules win over Tailwind's layered utilities, and `clip` doesn't create a scroll container. The new story spec must assert that a sheet really pins. Each scene is a section with a sticky, full height sheet inside it and some extra scroll length after it. The lengths, in viewport heights, are 150 for Try it on, 150 for Live store, 150 for AI operations, 200 for The product and 150 for Results. Each section is 100vh plus its length plus another 100vh tall, except Results, which is 100vh plus its length. Every section after the first overlaps the one before it by 100vh, and the z index rises by one per scene. So the next sheet slides up over the current one during the last 100vh. Beats sit at fractions of each scene's length. Try it on has 0, 0.4, 0.7 and 1. Live store and AI operations have 0, 1/3, 2/3 and 1. The product has 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1. Results has 0, 1/3, 2/3 and 1. That makes 21 beats, plus one stop at the start of the rest of the page. The prototype places an invisible marker at each beat and measures the markers to get the stop positions. Measure again when fonts load, when the viewport resizes and 700ms after mount.

The fit rules. Scene blocks keep their designed inner size and are scaled as a whole, always clamped between 0.45 and 1.12. On desktop the hero stage is 1100px wide and scales by `min((vw - 170) / 1100, (vh - 246) / stageHeight)`. The store, operations and product blocks are 1200px wide and scale by `min((vw - 256) / 1200, (vh - 104) / blockHeight)`, placed `86 + max(0, (vh - 104 - blockHeight x fit) / 2)` from the top. The result cards scale by `(vh - captionBottom - 86) / (cardHeight x 1.16)`. On phones the blocks are 350px wide and scale by `min((vw - 24) / 350, (vh - 108) / blockHeight)` clamped between 0.45 and 1.1. The stage scales by `min((vw - 40) / 350, (vh - 210) / stageHeight)` clamped between 0.45 and 1. On tablets, use the 560px column width in place of `vw`. The exact code is `directorMeasure` in `design/prototype/logic/director11.js`.

How a gesture becomes a beat. The story is controlled while the page is above the start of the rest of the page, and also when the visitor is exactly at the rest and scrolling up. While controlled, wheel, keys and touch are handled like this. A wheel event is prevented, ignored if Ctrl is held or the movement is mostly sideways, and scaled by 32 for line mode or by the viewport height for page mode. Deltas add up, and once they reach 24px in one direction the director moves one beat that way. The sum resets after 240ms of quiet. While a move is running, wheel events are ignored. After a move lands, a 170ms lock starts, and every event inside the lock extends it by 170ms. That swallows the long tail of a trackpad fling. The lock releases early if the gap between events passes 240ms or the magnitude jumps sharply, more than 1.6 times the previous delta and above 30, which is a new gesture. ArrowDown, PageDown and Space move forward. ArrowUp, PageUp and Shift plus Space move back. Keys are ignored when Alt, Ctrl or Meta is held, when focus is in a field, and for Space on a button or link. Home and End stay native. On touch, the mode is decided after 6px of movement. Sideways movement is left alone. Vertical movement inside the story is prevented and turned into one beat once it passes 34px. A tap that moved more than 10px swallows the click that follows for 450ms. Plain scrolling, including dragging the scrollbar, is allowed. 160ms after it stops, the page settles on the nearest stop, or enters the rest if it is past the story. Moves use a smooth scroll driven by requestAnimationFrame with a quartic in and out ease. A beat to beat move takes `clamp(420 + distance x 0.5, 700, 1300)` ms. A settle move takes `clamp(260 + distance x 0.7, 380, 900)` ms. On arrival the beat's own sequence starts and every timer from the previous beat is cleared. Below the story, input is native again. While the phone menu is open, the story ignores input.

Production changes to the director. Keep everything above, and add the following. Honour `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` by not pacing at all. The five scenes become ordinary sections one after another, with no sticky sheets and no overlap. Each shows its final beat, and its controls, tabs and list items still work by click. That is also what the golden full page screenshots will capture. Set `inert` on sheets that are fully covered, and on scenes that are off screen, so keyboard focus can't land behind the current sheet. Pause every timer and animation while the tab is hidden. Keep `scroll-behavior: auto` on the root while the director drives scrolling. When a nav item, rail button or hash link asks for a scene, move to it with the same tween. Use `100svh` sheets so mobile toolbars don't make the layout jump, and measure with the same unit the CSS uses. Never write per-frame values through React state. Write transforms, opacity and clip paths straight to refs, as the prototype does with its style cache. Keep React state for discrete things such as the current beat, captions, the active list item and counters.

How sheets look while moving. Scene backgrounds are the page background for Try it on, The product and Results, `--gc-sheet-store` for the Live store and `--gc-sheet-dark` for AI operations. As the next sheet comes in, its top corners start at a 30px radius and square off as it arrives. Its top shadow `0 -34px 80px -46px` in ink fades from 60% to nothing. The sheet underneath scales down by up to 6% around a point 38% from its top, rounds its corners by up to 30px and darkens under an ink veil of up to 26%. Results never recedes, and the rest of the page scrolls over it.

The rail and the phone progress bar. On desktop, a vertical rail near the right edge (the left in Arabic) lists the five scenes as 01 to 05. Each number has a 3 by 34px bar that fills with that scene's progress, and the current scene is at full opacity while the others are at 45%. The rail is cream on the dark AI operations scene and ink elsewhere. It fades and slides in once the hero has started moving and fades out when the rest of the page arrives. Hovering or focusing a number shows the scene name. Clicking 01 goes to the first try-on beat after the hero, and clicking any other number goes to that scene's first beat. On phones, the thin five segment bar under the header shows the same progress and can't be clicked. Make it turn cream on the dark scene too, which the prototype forgot.

Scene one, the hero and Try it on. The hero shows the eyebrow chip with three marks (AI try-on with the shirt icon, Store Chat with the chat icon and Shopify fashion stores with the Shopify mark), then the heading The fitting room your online store was missing., the subtitle, Try it on yourself and Book a call. Below it the product stage peeks up from the bottom, tilted back in 3D on a soft floor with a glow behind it. As the visitor moves into the scene, the hero text fades and rises by 70px. At the same time the stage rises, straightens and scales to fit. The tilt goes from 24 degrees to flat, 22 on phones, with a perspective of 1800px, or 1100 on phones. A soft sheen sweeps across once while it flattens. Then a caption line takes over above the stage, with a small chip reading 01 Try it on and four dots. The three beats after the hero each change the caption and play a short scripted demo with a visible pointer, moving forward only. In beat one the pointer taps the second garment and the look changes. In beat two it moves to the photo corner and locks the compare peek, so the shopper's original photo shows. In beat three it taps the next shopper, a man, and his look appears. 2.7s after the beat starts, the caption changes to Your turn. Pick a shopper and a garment. The pointer appears at 140ms, taps and changes the look at 1080ms and hides at 2300ms. Moving backward, or with reduced motion, shows the end state at once. If the visitor has already touched the stage, only the captions change.

The try-on stage itself. On desktop it is 1100px wide, with the try-on panel on the left (the right in Arabic) and the store chat panel beside it. On phones it is 350px wide with the try-on panel only. The chat moves to its own Ask the store section after the story. The try-on panel shows the current look in a 300 by 375 frame, 324 by 405 on phones, with an AI render tag. Six shopper avatars sit on a row of 40px buttons, and the chosen shopper's two garments are offered as picker cards. Choosing a garment wipes the new look in from the top in 0.95s with a thin sweep line. Until the visitor touches anything, the other garment shows a gentle invite ring. Hovering the frame with a mouse, or tapping it on touch, reveals the shopper's own photo so they can compare. Only three of the six shoppers have an original photo, and the frame hint only offers comparison for them. The twelve look images and their order are in the hero stage markup of `Scroll.dc.html`. The logic's `LOOK_SHOPPER` and `LOOK_NAMES` give the shopper and the garment name for each index. All of them are real outputs of the try-on engine on AI-generated models, and the small print under the stage says so.

The hero chat. It is a scripted, clearly labelled demo of Store Chat. It never calls an AI. The panel header shows the demo store avatar, GrindCTRL demo store, a status line reading Assistant may reply automatically, and an EN and عربي switch that changes only the chat's language and direction. The footer line reads Powered by GRINDCTRL. Demo conversation. The script, with its English and Arabic, is under `heroChatScript` in `copy/landing.json`. It starts with suggestion chips for the first question, the size guide and talking to a person. Asking the first question shows the shopper's message at once, a typing indicator at 380ms and the answer at 1700ms. It then offers the stock question and the size guide. The size guide answers at 1400ms. The stock question, or asking for a person, plays the handoff. Connecting you with our team appears at 1200ms. At 2100ms comes a small team inbox card showing Salma with an S avatar, marked Needs a reply, AI handed this off and Assigned to Omar. Our team joined the conversation appears at 3500ms, typing at 3900ms and the team's reply from Team · Omar at 5300ms. After that only Start over is offered. The first chip in each set is ink, the rest are outlined. On desktop the demo starts on its own 450ms after the visitor first reaches a try-on beat. On phones it starts when the Ask the store section is 35% visible. It never starts again once touched. The thread is a polite live region and the typing indicator has the status role.

Scene two, the live store. The heading reads A real store. Try it there. Next to it are three badges: a Shopify store badge with the Shopify mark, Try-on on every product and Store Chat. The whole viewport is one link to the matching page on the live store, opening in a new tab, with a small hint chip on hover and focus. The address bar shows the Shopify mark and the current path. The four beats go like this. Beat one is the home page with the chat bubble hint Hi, how can we help? Moving forward, the pointer glides to the Abayas tile at 900ms. Scene three, AI operations. This scene is dark. The heading reads Your team and the AI run support together. On desktop a list of four capabilities sits on the left: Answers from what you teach it, Order status inside the chat, Photos read, hard cases handed off and Your team takes over. Each has an icon tile: a book, the Shopify mark, a photo and the team avatars. On the right is a live graph of nine nodes, each a 150 by 64 card with its own mark. They are Store Chat, AI agent, Store knowledge, Shopify orders, Photo reader, Answer in chat, Hand off, Closed by AI and Team inbox. Below the graph sits a row of four counters: Conversations, Closed by AI, Handed to your team and Waiting now. Each beat makes one list item active, with a progress bar that fills in 4 seconds. It also lights one path through the graph, step by step, every 720ms. The active node turns cream and grows by 5%. Nodes off the path fade to 36%. Used edges are dashed until they are travelled, and the edge being travelled pulses. Beat one goes Store Chat, AI agent, Store knowledge, Answer and Closed by AI. Beat two goes through Shopify orders instead of knowledge. Beat three goes Store Chat, AI agent, Photo reader, Handoff and Team inbox. Beat four goes Handoff, Team inbox, Answer, and the AI node stays at 72% because the team has stepped in. The counters tick with a small pop. They go from 39, 26, 11 and 0 in beat one, adding one closed by AI. In beat three the handed and waiting counts rise to 12 and 1. In beat four waiting goes back to 0. On phones, the four capabilities become a segmented control, the path becomes a vertical five step stack with connectors that grow in, and the scene title is a polite live region. Scene four, the product. The heading reads One system, five connected parts. On desktop, five tabs on the left match the five beats: Storefront, Conversation, Customer, Workflow and Report. On the right, a product screen plays a short walkthrough for the current tab. It is rebuilt from the product's real screens and shown with demo data. Each walkthrough advances one frame every 800ms. The frame counts are 13, 15, 10, 13 and 11. The last frame holds for four ticks, then the walkthrough loops. The active tab has a progress bar for the same length. The Workflow tab can also play one of three cases once on request, Sizing question, Stock question or Photo of an order, which pauses the loop. On phones the tabs become a strip under the screen.

Scene five, results. The heading reads Same shopper, every garment. Eight look cards sit in a curved row, where the centre card is large and the neighbours shrink, fade and turn away. Each card shows a shopper in a garment with the name, the category and a Try it with your photo link to `/try-on`. Each beat brings a pair of cards to the centre. A card flips between the shopper's photo and the look with a clip wipe. Moving forward, the first card of the pair flips at 620ms, the second card centres at 1750ms and flips at 2400ms. For a card at distance d from the centre, x, scale and opacity are interpolated from these tables. On desktop x is 0, 338, 612, 860 and 1100, scale is 1.16, 0.9, 0.78, 0.7 and 0.7, and opacity is 1, 0.92, 0.5, 0 and 0. On phones x is 0, 250, 440, 600 and 760, scale is 0.86, 0.7, 0.6, 0.55 and 0.55, and opacity is 1, 0.85, 0.35, 0 and 0. The x values are multiplied by a spread of the viewport width over 1440, kept between 0.7 and 1.15, and on phones over 390, between 0.8 and 1.3. Each card turns by minus 8 degrees in Y for every step of distance, with the distance capped at 1.4, so the most any card turns is 11.2 degrees. Cards at a distance of 3 or more are hidden. A counter reads 01 / 08 with a thin fill. Tapping the centre card flips it, and tapping another card moves to its beat.

Blocks after the story rise 34px into place once as they enter the view, unless motion is reduced.

Header, menu and footer targets on this page. The desktop nav items move to scenes: Try it on goes to the first try-on beat after the hero, and Live store, AI operations, Product and Results go to their first beats. How it works scrolls to the How it works section, and Pricing opens `/pricing`. The logo goes back to the very top. The phone menu has Sign in first, then the same scene items (with The product instead of Product), Ask the store and Pricing. Picking a scene closes the menu and moves there. The footer items Product and Live store move to their scenes. Try-on page, Pricing, ROI calculator and Security open their routes. Every Book a call opens `BOOKING_URL` in a new tab and is tracked. Every Try it on yourself goes to `/try-on` and is tracked as `try_on`.

Switching language mid-story. The visitor stays on the same beat. Scene state such as the chosen shopper and look, the active tab and the counters is kept. The chat restarts in the new language. The current beat is shown in its end state rather than replaying its pointer. All positions are measured again after the text changes, because Arabic lines break differently.
