03, the try-on page

The public try-on page at `/try-on` is a small studio. The visitor picks a piece and adds a photo, and the page makes their look. The whole idea fits in one line, piece plus photo equals look, and the layout draws exactly that: a piece card, a plus, a photo card, an equals sign and the look card. The screenshots in `design/screens/tryon` walk through every state in all four layouts. The prototype's logic is in `design/prototype/logic/tryon11.js`, and its markup is in `design/prototype/generators/tryon11.py`. In the prototype every look is a stored image. In production, uploaded photos go through the real try-on pipeline that already powers the current page.

Keep the embed safe. `TryOnDemo`, `TryOnLocaleProvider` and `TryOnResult` are also used by the merchant storefront iframe at `app/embed/try-on/page.tsx`. Don't restyle or restructure them for this page. Build the new page from new components in `components/try-on/page/`. Share only logic: the API client calls, `lib/try-on/poll-policy.ts`, `lib/try-on/validator.ts`, `lib/try-on/types.ts` for the upload rules, `lib/try-on/shopper-errors.ts` for failure messages and `lib/try-on/i18n.ts` for existing copy. If the client calls currently live inside `TryOnDemo`, lift them into a small `lib/try-on/client.ts` that both use, and prove the embed still works with its existing tests. `app/try-on/page.tsx` keeps resolving the locale the way it does today, and it can use the shared `readSiteLocale` and `persistSiteLocale` helpers for switching.

Under the studio is the action bar, then the Your looks tray once there is a look, then the demo store strip, then a one line note that sample looks are real engine outputs on AI-generated models, then the footer. The journey. The four steps are The piece, Your photo, Create and Your look, with a hanger, a camera, a sparkle and a person icon. The photo card. With no photo, a large dashed drop area invites the visitor to add a photo. It reads Add your photo, then shows the upload rules. Under it is an or divider and a button: Use a sample photo, An AI-generated model, with a small thumbnail of that model. With a photo, the card shows it at 360px tall, cover fitted and focused 18% from the top. A tag reads Your photo with a camera for an upload, or Sample photo with a sparkle for a sample. Under the photo sits the privacy line with a shield icon. Use the existing privacy sentence from `lib/try-on/i18n.ts` (Your photo is used only to create this try-on preview. The look card. With no photo it shows a soft silhouette and Your look appears here. The card has `aria-live="polite"` and a scroll margin of 90px.

How a look is made, and the one intended change from the prototype. There are two paths. With a sample photo, the page shows the stored look for that piece straight away, with the reveal animation and no progress steps. These looks are real engine outputs made earlier on the sample models, so there is nothing to wait for and nothing to pretend. The mapping is `woman-abaya.webp` and `woman-linen-shirt.webp` on `shopper-woman.webp`, and `man-denim-overshirt.webp` and `man-knit-polo.webp` on `shopper-man.webp`. The caption reads A sample look made by the try-on engine. The preview is visual guidance, not an exact sizing guarantee. With an uploaded photo, the page runs the real pipeline. It creates a session with `POST /api/try-on/session`, body `{ productId, context: 'public-demo' }`. Create it when the visitor presses Create my look, or again when the piece changes, because a session belongs to one product. Then it records an attempt with `POST /api/try-on/attempt`, which gives this press a fixed request key so a retry never counts twice. Then it calls `POST /api/try-on/generate` with only `sessionId`, `attemptId`, `productId`, `photoSource: 'upload'`, a `photoReference` and the photo as a data URL in `photoData`. A public demo session must not send `garmentUrl` or `productName`. The route rejects a garment URL for public demo sessions, and the server reads the garment image from `public/` through the product's `imageUrl` and takes the name from the product. If the job comes back queued or processing, it polls `GET /api/try-on/jobs/{jobId}` with the session as a bearer token, using `lib/try-on/poll-policy.ts` for timing and honouring `Retry-After`. While it waits, the look card shows the photo blurred with a scanning band. Over it sits a panel headed Creating your preview with a small spinner, the five existing steps from `lib/try-on/i18n.ts` and a thin progress bar. The steps are Preparing your preview, Analyzing photo composition, Mapping product to your photo, Rendering try-on preview and Finalizing result. Steps advance every 820ms as a rhythm, hold on the last one until the result really arrives, and never claim to be finished early. When the result arrives, it replaces the blurred photo with the reveal, the caption changes to the same guidance line, and `storefront.demo_completed` is sent through the marketing helper described in `01-system.md`. `storefront.demo_started` goes out when Create my look is pressed. If the visitor changes the piece or the photo while waiting, abort the request and ignore any late answer.

When something fails. The design has no failure state, so add one inside the look card, in the same visual language. Show the soft silhouette, an alert icon, the existing `errorTitle`, a short localized message and a primary Try again button (the existing `tryAgain` copy) that reuses the same photo and piece. It also offers the two ways forward that always work: use a sample photo, or open the demo store. A failed generation usually arrives as HTTP 200 with `ok: true`, `status: 'failed'` and an English `message`, because the server doesn't know the visitor's language. Classify the failure on the client and show the matching localized sentence from `lib/try-on/i18n.ts` and `lib/try-on/shopper-errors.ts`, never the server's text as it is. A 429 waits for `Retry-After` before Try again is enabled. A 503 means the service isn't configured in this environment, so show the unavailable message rather than a crash. Retry once on its own after a network error or a 5xx, as the current page does, before showing the failure. `TRYON_UNAVAILABLE` only happens on storefront sessions, because the public demo doesn't spend credits, but handle it the same way in case that changes. Never show provider names, costs or raw error text. The generate response includes `meta.provider` and `meta.costEstimate`, and they must never reach the screen.

Automatic steps. The newest look is always what the visitor sees. When making starts and when it finishes, bring the look card into view if less than 80% of it is visible. Scroll it to the top of the screen if it is taller than the viewport minus 100px, and otherwise centre it. Scroll smoothly unless motion is reduced. With a sample photo, picking a different piece also switches the sample model to that piece's model, the woman for the abaya and the linen shirt and the man for the denim overshirt and the knit polo, and shows the stored look at once. With an uploaded photo, picking a different piece keeps the photo and waits for Create my look again.

The action bar. With no photo it shows a quiet hint: Add a photo to create your look. With a photo it shows Create my look, 56px tall, with a sparkle, full width on phones. With a look shown it offers Try another piece as the primary button, which opens the picker. It also offers Compare looks once there are two looks, Download, and Continue shopping, which opens the demo store. Download saves the shown look. A stored sample downloads its file. A generated look downloads the data URL as `grindctrl-look` with the right extension for its type.

The looks tray. Headed Your looks with a count of n of 4, it is a sideways scrolling row, newest first. Each item is a 72 by 90 thumbnail, 64 by 80 on phones, with the piece name under it. The shown look has a 2px ink ring and `aria-pressed`. Keep at most four looks, dropping the oldest. Each remembers its piece, its photo and its image, so choosing one from the tray restores all three. That includes an uploaded photo, which the prototype couldn't do.

The piece picker and the compare view. The picker is a dialog titled Try another piece, 460px wide and centred on desktop, and a bottom sheet with a 10px inset on phones. It has four rows, each with a 58 by 72 garment thumbnail, the name and the kind. The current piece has an ink ring and a tick. Compare is a dialog titled Your looks side by side, 760px wide, showing the look on screen next to the most recent other look, each with its name. Both dialogs need focus moved in, focus kept inside, Escape to close, focus returned to the opener and the page behind locked. The prototype had none of that.

The demo store strip. Near the bottom, a section tells visitors the same try-on runs inside a real store. It shows the Shopify mark with `grindctrl.myshopify.com`, the heading The same try-on, inside a real store., a Try it on with AI on every product badge, the Open the demo store button, the password chip and a grid of four real store products. The grid is four columns on desktop and two on phones, with 4 by 5 images. Each product links to its page on the store in a new tab: Riyadh Tie-Waist Open Abaya at $95.00, Blacktop Ringer Tee at $31.00, Heavyweight Boxy Tee at $28.00 and Boxy Tee and Short Set at $68.00. Product names and prices stay in English in both languages. Their images are the `st-` files in `assets/images`. The store buttons are tracked with the `try_on_store` placement.
