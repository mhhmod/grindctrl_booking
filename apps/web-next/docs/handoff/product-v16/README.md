GrindCTRL product v16, handoff for Claude Code

This folder holds everything needed to build the v16 redesign of the GrindCTRL product. It covers four surfaces. Try-On and Store Chat run on the merchant's Shopify store for shoppers. The merchant dashboard runs on grindctrl.cloud for owners and their team. The GrindCTRL app runs inside Shopify admin. The aim the owner set is an experience that feels effortless and premium for every role, at least as good as every competitor studied, and better in the ways that matter here: honest states, Arabic as an equal, and try-on and chat working as one product. Read this file first, then the nine numbered notes in order. They are written as plain paragraphs on purpose, so read them top to bottom.

The design lives on a Claude Design canvas the owner can open, called GrindCTRL Product Journeys, at version 23. It has 38 English boards and 36 Arabic twins, 74 boards in all. The English boards are Main, 10 for the shopper try-on, 8 for Store Chat with Chat-Arabic among them, 14 for the merchant dashboard and 5 for the Shopify admin app. Every English board except Main and Chat-Arabic has a twin named after it with `-AR`, such as `Today-AR`, which is the same screen in Arabic and right to left. The twins sit on four Arabic pages of the canvas. Main has an English and العربية switch that points every link at the twins, and each board's language switch opens its twin and back. The hand-made boards TryOn-Arabic, Today-Arabic and Shopify-Arabic are retired, because the twins replace them. Chat-Arabic stays, as the story of a shopper writing in Arabic and Franco. You can't open the canvas, so this folder carries what you need from it. `design/screens` has screenshots of every board, grouped by surface and numbered in journey order, the English boards in `design/screens/<surface>/` and their twins in `design/screens/<surface>/ar/` under the same file names. Shopper boards are phone screens at twice the pixel density, and boards that show several phones show them side by side. Dashboard and Shopify boards are full pages at 1440 pixels in `desktop` and at 390 pixels in `phone`. Interactive boards also have their states after a click, named after the state, for example `05-TryOn-Result.colour.webp` or `10-Plan.bought.webp`. The `start` folder has the canvas's index board, which shows how the journeys connect. `design/prototype/boards` holds all 74 built canvas files with their full markup and their small logic classes. `design/prototype/tools` holds the prototype tooling, kept for reference only: the scripts in `tools/arabic` that build each Arabic twin from its English board and the strings in `copy`, and the audit harness that checked every board at every width in both directions. `blob-to-file.json` maps each `/_blob/<id>` image path to a file in `assets/images`, and `canvas.json` lists every board with its size and title. Treat the prototype as a precise reference for sizes, spacing, copy and behaviour. None of it ships. The canvas tags (`x-dc`, `helmet`, `sc-if`, `sc-for` and the `{{ }}` holes), the Google Fonts links, the Review switch and the hard-coded sample data are prototype mechanics.

`assets/images` has the 14 images the boards use, and `assets/manifest.json` gives each one's role and the boards it appears on. None of them ship. People and products belong to a fictional store, and the try-on outputs stand in for looks the engine makes at run time. `assets/icons` has the line icons the boards use as standalone SVGs. The `copy` folder holds every visible and assistive string, per surface and board by board in reading order, in `start.json`, `try-on.json`, `store-chat.json`, `dashboard.json` and `shopify-app.json`. That includes the strings that only appear after an interaction, the dashboard's shared sidebar, and the Shopify chrome kept apart from our own. Each string carries its English as `en`, its Arabic as `ar`, its `kind`, and a `status` that is `approved` or `draft`. Every string has Arabic, the same Arabic the twins show. 149 pairs are approved by the owner, and they are also in `copy/arabic-approved.json` as before, each paired with its English. The rest are drafts for the owner's review. `tokens.json` maps every design value to the tokens in `app/globals.css`, names the few new ones with dark values and contrast ratios, and lists the storefront variables and the Polaris mapping.

The notes are these. `01-system.md` covers what every surface shares: roles, the eight design rules, libraries, tokens, type and icons, language and right to left, copy rules, numbers and dates, how data reaches a screen, the Review switch, the settings and preview models, accessibility, responsive behaviour, performance, events, privacy, dark mode and sample data. `02-try-on.md` covers the shopper try-on. `03-store-chat.md` covers Store Chat for shoppers. `04-dashboard.md` covers the merchant dashboard. `05-shopify-app.md` covers the app inside Shopify admin. `06-phase-zero.md` lists the 34 backend, correctness and safety items the screens depend on, in three bands, each checked against `main` at b92e71b with file and line evidence, the fix and its tests. `07-board-map.md` maps each board to the code it replaces or extends, the data behind every number, and what conflicts with it today. `08-qa.md` says what counts as done, which checks to add, including the responsive audit, and what has to be checked by hand. `09-backend-contracts.md` maps every screen to the routes, tables and fields that exist today, and gives an exact contract, with a code such as N1, for every feature the boards mark as new. `CLAUDE_CODE_PROMPT.md` is the prompt the owner pastes to start the work, and `PROGRESS.md` is where you record how far the work has got.

Every board has a Review switch, a canvas setting called `markNew` that is off by default. When it is on, every element that needs new back end work gets an orange dashed outline, and a note on the board explains it. In the markup those elements carry `data-be="new"`. The outlined elements are exactly the new back end work. Everything without an outline runs on what the app has today, its existing routes, tables, fields, options and flows, so build those parts on what exists. `09-backend-contracts.md` gives each outlined feature its code, its exact contract and its flag.

The sample store on the boards is Maison Noor, a fictional modest wear shop in Cairo. Its owner is Hana Adel, Nour Samir is on the team, and every number, order and price you see belongs to that sample. The plan numbers on the boards now come from the app's catalogue, the fallback values in `lib/try-on/public-catalog.ts`: Free with 15 looks a month, Launch at $15 for 150, Growth at $29 for 350, and Pro at $49 for 650 with hands-on setup and support. Every plan includes Store Chat, and the Boost 80 pack adds 80 looks for $5, valid 365 days and used after the plan's looks. Maison Noor is on Growth, with 150 looks left of 350 in the cycle from 18 September to 18 October. At about 14 looks a day it runs out about 12 October, 6 days before the reset. The other sample numbers were scaled to fit, such as 98 looks and 140 chats in the last 7 days and 420 looks in the last 30 days. The catalogue reads the live plan rows from the database first, and the repository's older seed SQL has Free 20 and Launch $15 for 300, so the live rows can differ from the fallback. Render every price, plan size and allowance from the live catalogue, never from the boards.

The owner has settled these points. The scope is all four surfaces and all 74 boards, the 38 English boards and their 36 Arabic twins. Phase zero comes first, and no shopper-facing part of v16 ships before band one is done. The boards are the visual and behavioural target. The words "looks" for the unit and "Try it on" for the shopper action are fixed. Arabic has equal standing, with Western digits. Shopper surfaces wear the merchant's theme. Nothing a screen shows may be faked: an element whose data does not exist yet is left out until it does. The v15 decisions still stand, including the `gc-locale` cookie as the only source of language on grindctrl.cloud.

Where the design and a repo rule disagree, the repo rule wins on truthfulness, accessibility, tokens, logical properties, Mantine for the SaaS surfaces, self-hosted fonts, the shared rate limit helpers, Clerk ownership checks and everything else in `AGENTS.md`. The design wins on layout, content order, visuals, interaction and copy. Phase zero wins over a board whenever a board shows something the back end cannot do yet. Where the repo has an older rule or test that v16 replaces on purpose, update it in the same change and say why. Never loosen a check to make it pass.

Work in this order. Pushing to `main` deploys to production, so everything goes through pull requests from `main`, each small enough to review and safe to merge on its own. Commit messages follow the repo's Conventional Commits style with a scope, such as `fix(try-on)`, `fix(store-chat)`, `feat(dashboard)` or `feat(shopify)`. Start with band one of phase zero, as separate pull requests. First come items 1, 3 and 4, one pull request each, which keep the first busy store from breaking. Item 1 can only use what exists today, so its launch answer comes from the install, the plan status and the looks left. Shape the field so it takes the launch state and the product pause when items 9 and 18 land. Then item 5, then item 2. Then items 6, 7 and 8 together, then items 10 and 11, then item 9. Next comes a foundations pull request: the new tokens and the Mantine bridge, the icon set, the dashboard shell and its routes and redirects, one active store (item 33), roles (item 12), and the copy and time zone helpers with their CI scans (item 32). Then build the surfaces in this order, each with the band two and three items it needs: the shopper try-on, Store Chat, the dashboard pages, and last the Shopify admin app. Ship new screens behind a per-store flag, so the owner can turn v16 on for the demo store first and check it in production, since there is no staging. Build every element the boards outline as new only with its contract from `09-backend-contracts.md` and behind its own flag. Keep `PROGRESS.md` current at the end of every pull request.

The work is done when every board is built and matches its screenshots at 390 and 1440 pixels, the English board in English and its twin in Arabic. It must hold together from 320 to 1920 pixels with no sideways scroll and pass the responsive audit in `08-qa.md`, and every number must be real and every action must work. Every check in `08-qa.md` must pass and CI must be green. Every pull request carries before and after screenshots, the list of Arabic strings it ships that are still drafts, the owner's open questions with the defaults it built, and the exact steps the owner takes after merging.
