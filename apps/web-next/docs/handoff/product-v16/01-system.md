This note covers what every v16 surface shares: who the product is for, the rules the boards follow, which library each surface uses, tokens, type and icons, language and right to left, copy, numbers and dates, how data reaches the screen, the Review switch, the settings and preview models, accessibility, responsive behaviour, performance, events, privacy, dark mode and sample data. Read it before any of the surface notes, because they lean on it instead of repeating it.

v16 redesigns four surfaces at once. The shopper try-on lives on the merchant's own store, drawn by the theme app extension in `apps/grindctrl-tryon/extensions/tryon-block` and the embed at `/embed/try-on` in `apps/web-next`. Store Chat also lives on the merchant's store, drawn by the loader in `apps/web-next/public/widget/v1/messenger.js` and the embed at `/embed/messenger`. The merchant dashboard is `apps/web-next/app/dashboard` on grindctrl.cloud. The Shopify admin app is `apps/web-next/app/shopify/app`, embedded in Shopify admin. `07-board-map.md` maps every board to these files in detail. There are four kinds of people. The shopper is anyone browsing a store, on a phone most of the time, often arriving from Instagram or TikTok, often writing in Egyptian Arabic or in Franco. The owner runs the store and pays for GrindCTRL. In the sample she is Hana Adel. A team member answers shoppers, keeps answers up to date, and never sees billing. The roles are today's Owner, Admin and Member: owners see everything, including billing, admins manage settings, and members answer chats and keep answers up to date. In the sample Nour Samir is a Member. The merchant in Shopify admin is the owner or a staff member who meets GrindCTRL first inside Shopify and may never visit grindctrl.cloud.

Eight rules shaped every board, and they decide the cases the boards don't show. Each screen has one obvious next step, and everything else stays quiet. Every state is honest. A wait shows real stages and a real estimate, a failure says why and offers a next step, and nothing promises what the back end does not do. Phase zero exists to make the promises true, so a board element whose data does not exist yet is left out until it does, never faked. Nobody is asked twice. The photo, consent, contact details and order context are remembered for the visit. Arabic is equal to English, not a translation pass at the end. Every product screen exists in Arabic and right to left, through the twins and Chat-Arabic, digits stay Western, and the Arabic is written for how people in Egypt and the Gulf actually speak. Shopper surfaces wear the merchant's theme and never the GrindCTRL palette or brand. Try-on and chat are one system. A chat product card has Try it on, a finished look has Ask about the fit, and the inbox shows the look a shopper chose to send. The merchant's home is Today, which shows only what needs a person, ranked by the money at stake, with one action per row. Setup checks itself, every editor has a live preview beside it, and running out of looks never comes as a surprise. Finally, everything is light and calm: scripts load late, skeletons hold the layout, nothing shifts, and floating layers never stack on top of each other.

Each surface uses the library `AGENTS.md` assigns to it. The dashboard is Mantine 9, mounted through `components/mantine/provider.tsx`, with every brand value coming from `components/mantine/theme.ts`. Almost every dashboard file is still shadcn today, so v16 is the moment to move the shell and the new pages to Mantine. Existing shadcn primitives may keep working inside a page you are not otherwise changing. The storefront embeds stay dependency light, with no UI library, no Mantine CSS and no Tailwind runtime, because they load on someone else's store. The Shopify admin pages are drawn in the Polaris look. `AGENTS.md` lists the embedded admin under Mantine 9, and phase zero item 28 asks the owner to confirm the move to Polaris web components, which is what Built for Shopify expects. Until the owner answers, build the admin pages in a way that keeps the choice open. Put the data loading, actions and copy in plain modules, and keep the rendering layer thin.

Colours come only from tokens. `tokens.json` maps every value on the dashboard boards to the tokens in `app/globals.css`. It also lists the new tokens v16 needs with their dark values: a raised panel colour and the status pairs for success, attention, danger and the count badge. Info and assistant colours reuse `--gc-input-icon-*` and `--gc-output-icon-*`. Add the new tokens to both themes in `globals.css`, bridge them in `components/mantine/theme.ts`, and extend `theme.test.ts` so the bridge cannot drift. The contrast ratios in `tokens.json` were computed from the oklch values. The repo's `--muted-foreground` passes AA on every surface the boards put it on, so keep it rather than adding the boards' slightly darker value. The count badge must use the darker orange given there, because white on the brighter one fails. Status is never carried by colour alone. Every badge has a word, and every dot has a label next to it.

The dashboard is set in Manrope, with IBM Plex Sans Arabic for Arabic, both already self-hosted (`--font-sans` and `--font-arabic`). The boards load Google Fonts only because the prototype needs them. `AGENTS.md` forbids external font links, so nothing in the product may load fonts from Google. The shopper widgets use the store's own font, read by the theme probe described in `07-board-map.md`. They fall back to the system stack and never to Manrope. For Arabic they use the theme's Arabic font when it has one, and otherwise IBM Plex Sans Arabic served from grindctrl.cloud. The type scale is in `tokens.json`. In short, page titles are 30 pixels at weight 800, section titles 18 at 800, body 14 to 15, supporting text 13.5, and nothing is smaller than 12. Figures use tabular numbers everywhere. Small capital labels are stored in sentence case in the copy files and uppercased with CSS in English only, never in Arabic.

The boards draw a line icon set. `assets/icons` has every icon the boards use as a standalone SVG on a 24 grid with `currentColor` strokes. In the dashboard, use the matching icon from the repo's icon library (Hugeicons, per `components.json`) and fall back to these files only where no match exists. In the storefront widgets, inline the few icons they need from this folder, since the widgets carry no icon library. `shopify.svg` and `wa.svg` are simplified stand-ins. Use the official Shopify and WhatsApp marks from each brand's resources and follow their rules. Directional icons such as chevrons, back arrows and send flip in right to left layouts. Icons that are not directional, such as the flag, the person or the camera, do not.

Language works like this. On grindctrl.cloud the `gc-locale` cookie stays the only source of truth, as in v15. The dashboard's language switch sets it and updates `html` `lang` and `dir` at once. In the Shopify admin the language follows the admin's `locale` parameter, which the page already reads. In the shopper widgets it follows the store page's language, passed by the loader. Store Chat's header also has its own Arabic and English switch for the panel chrome, as Chat-Arabic shows. The assistant replies in the shopper's language when the merchant leaves Reply language on Match the shopper, as it does today, and following her dialect and answering Franco in Franco are new. Every string has Arabic. Each English board except Main and Chat-Arabic has an Arabic twin that shows the same screen right to left, and the `copy` folder carries each string's English and Arabic with its status, approved or draft. 149 pairs are approved by the owner. The rest are drafts for the owner's review, so a pull request lists the drafts it ships. The twins also set the voice. On the sample store the shopper is addressed in the feminine, the dashboard uses simple Modern Standard Arabic, and the Shopify admin uses neutral admin Arabic. Digits stay Western everywhere, including inside sentences, and prices read "3,250 ج.م". Every layout uses logical properties (`margin-inline-start`, `inset-inline-end`, `padding-inline`) so right to left needs no separate CSS, and directional icons flip. Text that a person typed, such as chat messages, product names, search input and the composer, gets `dir="auto"` so mixed text reads correctly. Numbers, codes, URLs, email addresses and phone numbers inside Arabic text are isolated left to right with `bdi` or a `dir="ltr"` span, so "K7QM-4TXP", "+20 100 000 0000" and "maisonnoor.com" keep their order. A CI scan for Eastern digits is part of phase zero item 32.

Arabic has grammatical gender, and the boards handle it on purpose. On shopper surfaces the store chooses how its Arabic addresses shoppers: women, men or everyone. The setting sits on ChatStudio and Shopify-Chat ("In Arabic, speak to shoppers as"), applies to both widgets, and is new back end work. The sample store sells modest wear and uses the feminine forms you see on the shopper twins and Chat-Arabic, such as "جربيها عليكِ", "غيّري الصورة" and "اسألي عن المقاس". The everyone setting needs neutral phrasing, which the boards don't show. Write it, mark it as a draft, and list it for the owner. On the dashboard, a person's form of address comes from an explicit profile setting, never from a guess based on their name. Without the setting, use neutral forms. Today-AR greets the owner by name and gives her role in the feminine ("مساء الخير يا هناء", "المالكة") because the sample owner chose that form. Apart from that, the dashboard twins use simple Modern Standard Arabic and the Shopify twins use neutral admin Arabic.

Copy rules. Every string on the boards is in the `copy` folder in reading order, with assistive text and the strings that only appear after an interaction, each with its English, its Arabic, its kind and a status of approved or draft. The approved Arabic is also in `copy/arabic-approved.json`, paired with its English. Use it exactly, and use it as the model for tone and terms everywhere else. Use the drafts as written, keep the ones a pull request ships in one list, and mark them for the owner's review. For a string no board shows, draft the Arabic in the same voice and add it to that list. The unit is looks, everywhere. Never say credits, renders or generations to a merchant or a shopper. The shopper action is Try it on. There are no em dashes in copy, and no promises of instant replies. Everything the assistant writes or the try-on makes carries a visible AI label ("AI preview", "Store assistant, AI"). Every claim about the product goes through `lib/product-truth`. Prices, plan sizes and the free allowance come from the live catalogue, never from the boards. The boards show the fallback values of `lib/try-on/public-catalog.ts`, and the live plan rows can differ (phase zero item 21). Names of people, products and the store in the boards are sample content. Strings like "The team usually replies in about 10 minutes" and "Looks run out in about 10 days" are templates whose numbers come from data, and the sentence is only shown when the data exists.

Numbers, dates and money follow the store. Money uses Shopify's money format for the store's currency, rendered with Western digits, for example "EGP 3,250" in English and "3,250 ج.م" in Arabic. Dates are short and spoken, such as "Friday, 2 October", "12 Sep" or "Today, 11:20 am". Relative times such as "3 min ago" and "12 min" update while the page is open. Days, weeks and "today" are counted in the store's time zone, not UTC (phase zero item 32). Opening hours are shown in the store's time zone with the zone named once where a shopper might be elsewhere ("10 am Cairo time"). Rates show whole percentages unless a decimal changes the meaning ("6.1%" against "5.6%" on the 50/50 card). Estimates always say "about" and come from measurements, for example the median look time over the store's recent looks. With too few measurements to be honest, leave the number out.

How data reaches a screen. Every figure on every board has a source, named per board in `07-board-map.md` and per backend item in `06-phase-zero.md`. Build each data block with four states: loading as a skeleton the same size as the content, ready, empty with the plain sentence the board gives for the quiet case, and failed. A failed block says it could not load and offers Try again. It never shows calm zeros, which is what the current overview does (`overview-data.ts:245`). Blocks that show a check say when it ran ("Checked in your live theme 3 min ago") and offer Check again. Counts that move during the day refresh on their own, without a full page reload. Inbox and the waiting count update live. Today's health strip and Right now refresh about once a minute, and the rest refreshes when the page regains focus. The Inbox count in the nav, the looks meter in the sidebar and the bell must agree with the pages they lead to, so take each from one shared query.

The Review switch. Every board has a Review switch, the canvas setting `markNew`, which is off by default. When it is on, every element that needs new back end work gets an orange dashed outline, marked `data-be="new"` in the board's markup, and a note on the board explains the outline. The outlined elements are exactly the new back end work. Everything without an outline runs on what the app has today, its routes, tables, fields, options and enums, so build those parts on what exists and nothing else. `09-backend-contracts.md` maps every screen to the existing pieces and gives each outlined feature a code (N1, N2 and so on), an exact contract and a flag. Build an outlined element only with its contract and behind its flag, and leave it out of the screen until both exist, which is the rule against faking above. The outline is a review aid and never ships.

Settings follow the two models the app has today, and both keep the "one settings table" rule in `apps/web-next/PRODUCT.md`. Store Chat has a published version and at most one draft (`lib/messenger/actions-core.ts`). ChatStudio edits the draft, and while one exists a bar says how many changes are waiting, with "Your changes are saved. Shoppers still see the published version.", Review changes, Discard and "Publish to your store". "Revert to previous version" sits under the published version number. Try-On settings save straight away. TryOnStudio shows "1 unsaved change" with Discard and Save, and after a save "Saved, live within a minute.", which is today's behaviour and copy (`lib/try-on/dashboard-copy.ts:244`). In Shopify admin, the contextual save bar edits the same settings in the same way. On Shopify-TryOn, Save goes live within a minute. On Shopify-Chat, Save keeps the changes as the draft, and "Publish to your store" makes them live. Discard throws away the page's unsaved edits on both. The web and the admin edit the same rows, so a waiting Store Chat draft shows on both.

Every editor has a live preview beside it, and the previews are drawn by our own components, never by framing the merchant's live store. Storefronts usually refuse to be framed, and a framed store would also run the merchant's own scripts. The preview renders the real widget components with the store's real product, its theme tokens from the probe, the unsaved changes, and a state switcher (Bubble, Open, Answer, Team and After hours for chat; Button, Card pill, Photo, Making, Look and Problem for try-on), in either language. It is labelled as the store's real product page with the changes, which is what the board copy says. When the probe has not run yet, use the server-side theme snapshot, or neutral defaults with a note.

Accessibility is part of the design. Every tap target is at least 44 by 44 pixels in the shopper widgets and 40 by 40 on the dashboard, and Shopify admin controls are at least 32 pixels tall. Focus is always visible with `:focus-visible`. Every page has a skip link, landmarks and one `h1`. Dialogs and sheets trap focus, close on Escape, return focus to what opened them, and leave the page inert behind them. A closed widget iframe is removed from the tab order. Status changes that a person waits for, such as generation stages, a look becoming ready, a new chat message or a saved change, are announced through a polite live region. Images of looks have real alt text ("You wearing the Layla Embroidered Abaya in black"), and decorative images are hidden. Charts have a text alternative that states the takeaway, plus native per-point titles. Every animation stops under reduced motion. Forms label every field, show errors next to the field in words, and keep what the person typed. Run axe on every new page in both languages.

Responsive rules. Every board now meets one set of rules, and an audit checked each one at fixed sizes in both directions: phones at 320, 360, 375, 390, 414 and 430 pixels wide and at 375 by 667, pages at widths from 320 to 1920, and storefront desktops from 1024 by 768 to 1920 by 1080, once left to right and once with right to left forced. Build to the same rules. Nothing scrolls the page sideways. A row that does not fit wraps, or becomes a sideways scroller contained in its own track, like the tab rows, the chip rows and the Try these row on TryOn-Result. A view fixed to the phone's height scrolls only its middle, with its header and its composer or action bar pinned. Dialogs and sheets fit the viewport and scroll inside. Tap targets are at least 44 by 44 pixels for shoppers and 40 by 40 on the dashboard, and Shopify controls are at least 32 pixels tall. No text is smaller than 12 pixels, including previews, avatars and key hints. Inputs are as tall as their fields, so no text is clipped. Layouts use logical properties only, directional icons flip, and numbers, codes, URLs and phone numbers are isolated left to right. The audit allows a few intended cases: a modal over the page (the Command palette, the TryOn-Desktop dialog and the TryOn-Photo sheet at 375 by 667), single line ellipses that carry a `title` attribute (Inbox list previews, the product name in the Chat-Launcher sticky bar at 320 and the share URL on TryOn-More), message boxes inside a scrolling chat list at 375 by 667 (Chat-Answer, Chat-Order and Chat-Arabic), and an inline text link on TryOn-Check. Shopper widgets are designed at 390 pixels. On phones they open as bottom sheets, and on desktop as centred dialogs or a docked panel. The dashboard is designed at 1440. Use the Mantine breakpoints in `components/mantine/theme.ts` (sm 640, md 768, lg 1024 and xl 1280 pixels) rather than the prototype's, as `AGENTS.md` asks for Mantine work. The two-column layouts become one column and the KPI grid becomes two columns below 1024 pixels. The sidebar becomes the bottom tab bar from Owner-Phone below 768 pixels. Wide charts become the meter or list the phone boards show below 640 pixels. Tables become stacked rows on phones, with the first column as the row title. The Shopify admin pages follow Polaris layout and its breakpoints. `08-qa.md` turns these rules into checks.

Performance budgets. The storefront loader must stay small and must not block rendering. Create the try-on iframe only when a shopper taps, and preconnect on idle or hover. Create the chat iframe only when the panel opens. The product page must not shift. Band one's fix keeps the Try it on block hidden until config arrives. The v16 block then decides its visibility before the page paints, as `02-try-on.md` describes, and every widget reserves the space it needs before it appears. Dashboard pages render on the server, stream their slow blocks behind skeletons, and keep interaction delays low on a mid-range Android phone. The Shopify admin pages should pass Built for Shopify's web performance thresholds.

Events. Phase zero item 22 defines one event pipeline for both widgets, with a visitor id, the 50/50 bucket, the product and the visit source, plus a Web Pixel for add to cart and checkout. The surface notes name the events each step sends. Never put a shopper's photo, look or message text into an event. Test looks from the owner's preview are flagged and left out of every number (item 9).

Privacy rules that every surface must keep, from phase zero item 2. The shopper's photo goes to the try-on service, makes one look, and is then deleted. The photo and looks are kept on the shopper's phone for 24 hours, in our iframe's storage, only when the shopper leaves "Keep my photo on this phone" on. The store sees a look only when the shopper sends it, through Ask about the fit, Send to the store, or a chat attachment. A shopper's photo or look is drawn only inside our iframes, never in the merchant page's DOM, because session replay and analytics scripts on the store record that DOM. That applies to the "Your look" card on TryOn-Browse and to the floating chip, which shows the shopper's photo. WhatsApp messages go only to people who asked for them, and offers need a separate permission that is always required. Consent is asked once and remembered on the phone, apart from the photo and looks and with its own longer expiry, so the promise "We only ask this once" stays true after the 24 hours.

Dark mode. The dashboard boards are light, which is the designed theme, but the dashboard follows next-themes like the rest of the app and must stay legible in dark. That is why every new token has a dark value in `tokens.json`. Check each page in dark before calling it done, especially text on status fills and on the primary colour, as `AGENTS.md` warns. The shopper widgets follow the store's theme, which may itself be dark, so derive every widget colour from the probed values and check contrast against them. The Shopify admin follows Polaris.

Sample data. Maison Noor, its owner, team, products, orders, numbers and prices are fictional. They exist only to make the boards readable. Use them in tests, fixtures and Storybook-style stories if you add any, and never in production code paths, default values or empty states. The images in `assets/images` are sample content under the same rule. The try-on outputs among them are stand-ins for looks the engine makes at run time. The sample numbers were scaled to the Growth plan the sample store is on, so the boards agree with each other: 150 looks left of 350, about 14 looks a day, 98 looks and 140 chats in the last 7 days, 420 looks in the last 30 days, and 14 looks and 18 chats today. Fixtures that reproduce a board should use the same numbers.
