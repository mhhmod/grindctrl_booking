This note covers Store Chat as shoppers see it on the merchant's store. The boards are Chat-Launcher, Chat-Home, Chat-Answer, Chat-Order, Chat-Arabic, Chat-Desktop, Chat-States and Chat-More, with screenshots in `design/screens/store-chat`. `07-board-map.md` maps them to `public/widget/v1/messenger.js`, `app/embed/messenger` and `components/messenger`. The backend items they depend on in `06-phase-zero.md` are 5 (custom domains), 6 (handoff copy keyed on the alert), 7 (after hours), 8 (assistant down), 10 (escalation rules), 11 (retrieval of approved answers), 14 (WhatsApp), 15 (order lookup), 25 (catalogue, policies and structured answers), 31 (honest counts) and 32 (copy, launcher offset and accessibility). Every board except Chat-Arabic has an Arabic twin, such as Chat-Home-AR, with screenshots in `design/screens/store-chat/ar`, and Chat-Arabic stays as the story of a shopper writing in Arabic and Franco. Today the assistant answers only from the merchant's knowledge entries, the notes and the pages added by link, and it is off outside business hours. So the boards outline as new every answer that quotes stock, sizes or product details, product cards and adding to cart inside the chat, and every line that says the assistant keeps answering after hours.

What the shopper should feel. Store Chat answers like the best person on the shop floor. It is quick, it is specific to the piece she is looking at, and it speaks her language and dialect. It turns an answer into a next step, such as trying the piece on, adding the right size, tracking an order or reaching a person. It never pretends: it says it is an AI, says when a person will reply and when they won't, and never loses what she typed. It never gets in the way of shopping either. The bubble never covers Add to cart, and nothing pops up uninvited.

There are three ways in. The first is the product page row, Chat-Launcher, which is where most chats should start. Under the product details sits a quiet card: "Questions about this abaya?" with an "AI answers" label, three suggested questions as chips, and a field labelled "Ask about this abaya" with the placeholder "Ask anything about this piece" and a send button. The noun comes from the product type or a merchant setting, with "this piece" as the default. The suggested questions come from what shoppers really asked about this product or its type, plus gaps the catalogue can answer (items 11 and 25). With no data, show none rather than generic filler. Tapping a chip or sending opens the panel with that question already sent and the product in context. The row is new. It is an app block, or is drawn by the shared loader, and the merchant can turn it off ("Ask from the product page" on ChatStudio and Shopify-Chat).

The second way in is the bubble. It sits in the bottom corner the merchant picked, on every page except checkout. Where the theme has a sticky Add to cart bar on phones, the bubble sits above it. Detect a fixed or sticky element at the bottom of the viewport that holds an add to cart form or button, and lift the bubble by its height plus a margin. ChatStudio shows that as "sits 96 px higher there". While the product page row is on screen, the bubble steps aside, as ChatStudio's preview says. It comes back when the row scrolls away. The bubble and the try-on chip never overlap. When both are needed, the chip takes the bottom and the bubble sits above it. The third way in is from the try-on. "Ask about fit" on a look opens the panel with that look attached as the shopper's message. The first hello stays as it works today, a small bubble a few seconds after the page loads (6 seconds on the board), which the merchant sets on ChatStudio. Show at most one per visit, never on checkout, never over the cart bar, and never with the old "we reply instantly" line. Keeping it away while the product page line is visible is new.

The panel opens as a bottom sheet of about 92 percent height on phones and as a docked panel on desktop. On desktop it is 400 pixels wide on the bubble's side, as Chat-Desktop shows, and as tall as the window, over the page without dimming it, so the shopper can keep looking at the product. Today's docked panel is 384 by 600 pixels, so this is wider and full height. The board draws it below the store's header, which is fine when the loader can measure a sticky header; otherwise run it from top to bottom. It closes with the close button, Escape, a swipe down on phones and the back gesture. Focus moves into the composer when it opens and returns to the bubble or the row when it closes. While closed, the iframe is out of the tab order and hidden from screen readers. Create the iframe on the first open, not on page load.

The panel home, Chat-Home. The header shows the store's mark (initials when there is no logo), its name and a status line in two halves: "Assistant answers now · team here until 10 pm". The team half follows opening hours today, and the current shift once item 13 adds shifts. The assistant half is shown whenever the assistant will answer. Today the assistant is off outside business hours, so after hours the header can only say the team is away, and "Assistant answers now · team away until 10 am" is new (item 7). When it is down, only the team half remains, as on the third phone of Chat-States. The status dot is green only when someone or something will really answer now. A More options button and a close button sit at the end of the header. The board does not show the More options menu. Put the panel's secondary actions there, for example starting a new conversation, and draft their copy for the owner. Under the header, "You're viewing" shows the product from the page the shopper is on, with its colour, size and price and a Try it on button that opens the try-on sheet for that variant. Then comes the welcome screen the merchant wrote for this language, the title "Welcome to Maison Noor" and the message under it, which are today's welcome title and subtitle, with the "Store assistant, AI" label. Four quick actions follow, and they are new: Track my order, Sizes and fit, Delivery and exchange, and Talk to a person. Each sends a clear first message and is answered like any other, and Talk to a person sends today's handoff phrase, so it starts a handoff at once. Earlier conversations from this visitor show as rows ("Yesterday, Exchange for the Riyadh abaya") and open read only with a way to carry on. That row is new too. The composer has an add photo button, the placeholder "Ask about sizes, delivery or an order", which is the merchant's message box hint, a microphone that uses the browser's speech input where it exists and is hidden where it doesn't, and a send button. Under it sits the disclosure: "Answers come from the store's AI assistant. A person from the team takes over when you ask, or when it can't help."

An answer that sells, Chat-Answer. Once a product is in context, the header shows it with a back button to the home view. Replies stream in as they are written. Each assistant turn can carry structured parts from item 25: text, a table, a product card, sources and follow-up chips. The board's answer to "Is this in black in M? And how long is it on 160 cm?" leads with the direct answer in bold ("Yes, black is in stock in M, 3 left."). It explains in one or two sentences, then shows a table of size, length, length on her height and stock in her colour, with her size highlighted and sold-out sizes marked. A source chip says where the facts came from ("From the store's size guide"), then a product card has Try it on and "Add M". Below the turn are the "Store assistant, AI" label, Helpful and Not helpful buttons that feed Knowledge, and two or three follow-up chips ("Show me sand", "Delivery to Alexandria?", "Exchange rules"). Add M goes through the loader to the store's `/cart/add.js` and confirms in the chat without leaving the page. A sold-out size is never offered. The sentence "ends about 4 cm above your ankle" is a computed fit claim. It needs garment lengths in the size chart and a rule for estimating length on the body, so phase zero lists it for the owner. Until it is approved, answer with the chart's garment length and leave the on-body estimate out. The placeholder in this view is "Ask a follow-up". On the board the stock and length answer with its table, the source chip, the product card, Add M and the follow-up chips are new, because today the assistant answers only from the merchant's notes and pages. Helpful and Not helpful exist.

Orders, Chat-Order and Chat-More. A signed-in shopper who asks "Where is my order?" sees "Here are your recent orders, Salma. Tap one to see where it is.", her recent orders as cards (number, items, total, status), and the chosen order's detail. The detail shows the status badge, an arrival line only when the carrier gives one ("Arrives today by 9 pm"), the pieces, and a timeline (placed, shipped with the carrier, out for delivery) with dates. The board no longer has a "Confirmed by phone" step. "Cash due on delivery" with the amount appears for cash orders, along with "Track with Bosta" (the fulfilment's tracking link) and "Updates on WhatsApp" (opt-in through item 14). A guest, on the first phone of Chat-More, gets a small form inside the chat: "Order number" and "Email or phone you ordered with", "Find my order", and "We only show an order to the email or phone that placed it." Today the lookup matches the order number with the email on the order, so the email half works now and matching by phone is new (item 15). The form goes to a deterministic lookup endpoint, not through the model (item 15). It is rate limited per address and per order number. A wrong number and a wrong email or phone get the same plain sentence, which you should draft and flag. The second phone shows the result: the masked email in the shopper's message ("#1027 · m•••••@gmail.com"), "Found it. It's on its way with Bosta.", an order card with the timeline and the cash due, "Send me updates on WhatsApp", and "Store assistant, AI · it can read your order but not change it". The order views use the placeholder "Ask about this order". The assistant can read orders and never change them. The cards and the detail are drawn from the lookup's own result, not from the model, so totals and the amount due can be shown to a verified shopper as the boards do. Keep prices out of the facts given to the model until the owner widens that allowlist (`07-board-map.md`, Chat-Order). Today the lookup gives the assistant the status, the items and the tracking link, without prices or carrier events. So the order list, the structured order card, the amounts, the carrier events and WhatsApp updates are new. A change of address or an exchange is a handoff with the order attached (item 10).

Arabic, dialect and Franco, Chat-Arabic. The panel's chrome follows the store page's language, and the header carries its own "عربي" and "EN" switch, because many shoppers browse an English theme and write in Arabic. The assistant answers in the language and dialect the shopper writes in. The store's default dialect is set on ChatStudio ("Egyptian, follows the shopper"), and when a shopper writes Arabic in Latin letters ("tayeb law 3ayza a3'ayar el lon ba3d ma yewsal?"), it answers in Franco the same way, when the merchant leaves "Answer Franco in Franco" on. Every bubble and the composer get `dir="auto"`, so Arabic, English and Franco each sit the right way in one thread. Source chips and product card actions use the store's Arabic address setting. The sample store uses feminine forms ("جربيها عليكِ", "أضيفي M", "اسألي عن أي حاجة"), and those are approved. Draft the masculine and neutral forms and flag them. Digits stay Western. On the board the stock answer, the product card, the Franco reply and the policy chip are new, and so are the dialect, address and Franco settings.

Desktop, with try-on inside the chat, Chat-Desktop. When a shopper asks whether a piece suits her, the assistant can offer to show it on her: "Want to see it on you? I can use the photo saved on this device." That offer appears only when she kept her photo on this phone and agreed to the try-on terms (item 2). "See it on me" makes the look inside the conversation, with "AI preview", an expand button, the caption "Layla, Black, M. Made from your saved photo." and "Add to cart · Black, M". Both iframes come from grindctrl.cloud on the same store, so the chat can read the photo the try-on saved. The look itself goes through the try-on job API with a context fetched by the loader. Here is the rule that matters. A look made inside the chat is shown only to the shopper. It is not stored in the conversation the team can read, unless she sends it with Ask about the fit or by attaching it. The Inbox's "Look she sent you" depends on that. See it on me, the look inside the chat and adding to cart from the chat are new.

When a person is needed, Chat-States. The first phone is a real alert. The shopper asks for a person, the assistant confirms in one sentence that names what it passed on ("Of course. I've passed your message and order #1042 to the team."), and a green card says "The team has your message". Under it, "The team usually replies in about 10 minutes. You will see the reply here." gives the team's measured reply time and drops the time when there is no measurement. Then "If you leave, where should they reply?" offers Email, which is today's reply address capture, shown with her address and Edit, or WhatsApp, which is new (item 14). The assistant adds "While you wait, I can still help with anything else." This card appears only when someone was really alerted (item 6). Otherwise the after-hours card shows. The second phone is after hours. "The team is away until 10 am Cairo time" names the store's time zone, then comes "Tell us where to reply and they will answer first thing tomorrow.", a choice of Email or WhatsApp, the email field and "Send to the team". This is true today, because the assistant is off outside business hours and the merchant can ask for a reply address outside opening hours too. The WhatsApp choice, the "Assistant answers now" header and "Or keep asking me, I am here all night." are new (items 7 and 14). Nobody is alerted at night (item 7). The third phone is the assistant being down. The shopper's message is marked "Saved", and a card says "The assistant can't answer right now" and "Your message is saved. Send it to the team and they reply here, usually in about 10 minutes.", with "Send it to the team" and "Message the store on WhatsApp". The card is new (item 8), and its WhatsApp button needs item 14 as well. The fourth phone is a photo of a problem. The shopper sends a photo of a loose hem, and the assistant answers from the order and the store's policy without deciding anything itself: "I'm sorry about that. The stitching at the hem looks loose in your photo. The store's policy covers faults like this, and the team decides how to put it right. Tap below and I'll send them your photo and order #1019." Then comes "Ask for a new one or a refund", which starts a handoff with that reason, and "Store assistant, AI · the team sees your photo too". The assistant never promises a refund or a replacement, because those decisions belong to a person (item 10). Photo attachments and photo triage exist today. Attachments must be on for this, and the policy line must come from the store's own policy, never from a guess. That reply was reworded after the owner's last review, so list it among the strings for the owner to check.

Offline, the third phone of Chat-More. When the connection drops, a dark bar says "You're offline. We'll send it when you're back." Messages she sends wait in the conversation in a lighter style marked "Waiting to send", and a line above the composer says "Keep typing. Nothing you write is lost." The outbox lives in the iframe's storage, survives a reload, and sends in order with the same keys when the connection returns, so nothing is stored twice (item 8).

The rules for every reply. It answers first and asks later. It never invents a policy, a size, a stock count or a delivery date. When it doesn't know, it says so and offers the team (#36 already does part of this). It carries the AI label. It cites a source for facts from the store. It never offers a sold-out variant. It never makes a promise about the team that the alert result does not support. It never writes an em dash. The header status, the handoff cards and every "about 10 minutes" come from data. The composer placeholder follows the context: "Ask about sizes, delivery or an order" at home, which is the merchant's message box hint, "Ask a follow-up" after an answer, "Ask about this order" in an order, and "Message" everywhere else.

Events for item 22, with the visitor id, bucket and product: the row seen, a suggested question tapped, the panel opened (with where it was opened from), a message sent (with the language and script, never the text), a reply shown (with the parts it carried), Helpful or Not helpful, a product card's Try it on or Add, an order looked up (found or not), a handoff requested and its alert outcome, a WhatsApp opt-in, and the outbox sending after being offline.

Store Chat is done when, on a 390 pixel phone and a 1440 desktop, in English and Arabic, on a theme with a sticky Add to cart bar, the bubble never covers the button and the row opens a chat that answers about the piece on screen. Add must put the right variant in the cart. Track must work for a signed-in shopper and for a guest with order number and email, and with phone once item 15 lands. Each of the four handoff states must appear exactly when its condition is true. An offline message must arrive once. The panel must pass axe with focus handled. And no GrindCTRL colour or "Powered by" line may appear on the store.
