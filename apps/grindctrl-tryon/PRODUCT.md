# GrindCTRL Try-On

## Register

brand

## Users

The primary recipients are owners, founders, and ecommerce decision-makers at women’s fashion, dress, abaya, and modest-fashion stores. They usually encounter GrindCTRL through a short outbound email while reviewing store operations, merchandising, or growth opportunities. They need to understand the product quickly, trust the sender, and see a low-friction path to a relevant demonstration.

## Product Purpose

GrindCTRL Try-On adds an AI-assisted virtual try-on journey to product and catalog pages. It lets shoppers upload a photo and preview a selected fashion item without leaving the store. The campaign’s job is to turn relevant fashion-store prospects into qualified demo conversations by offering a private example made from one real product in the recipient’s collection.

## Brand Personality

Premium, editorial, and precise. The voice is confident without pressure, fashion-aware without ornament, and technically credible without jargon. The visual character should feel like a tailored black garment: controlled, distinctive, and immediately recognizable.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS campaign emails with blue gradients, decorative dashboards, nested cards, or stock illustrations.
- Luxury clichés such as excessive gold UI, ornamental serif typography, and vague prestige language.
- Broken or improvised email HTML, mojibake, missing images, clipped layouts, or styling that depends on unsupported browser CSS.
- Manipulative urgency, false scarcity, “last chance” language, pricing-led outreach, or deceptive subject lines.
- Dense copy that hides the one-product demo offer or presents several competing primary actions.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the product before explaining the platform.** Use the approved try-on journey image and one concrete product example to make the value tangible.
2. **One decisive response path.** “Reply DEMO” is the primary action; the live demo link is a quieter secondary path that can scale across campaigns.
3. **Practice the promised confidence.** Every email must render cleanly, preserve correct text encoding, and remain useful when images are unavailable.
4. **Personal, not automated.** Copy should read like a focused note to a specific store owner, not a mass newsletter.
5. **Follow up normally.** Each touch adds a useful angle without pressure, fake finality, or repeated filler.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for text contrast, meaningful alt text, readable type sizes, keyboard-accessible links, and clear hierarchy. Support Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail, desktop, and mobile layouts. Provide a plain-text alternative, avoid meaning that depends only on color or imagery, and preserve correct UTF-8 rendering for international names and future Arabic or bilingual variants.
